Splash Zone

A team installation that splashes vinyl type across a campus bathroom, turning an everyday room into a playful, slightly uncomfortable experience.

My role

Team project: concept, mockups and installation [add your specific part]

Timeline

4 weeks · Spring 2026

Course

Typography III, with Katherine Hughes

Tools

Illustrator, Photoshop, Procreate, Figma

The brief

What happens to type when you can walk into it?

Typography III asked us to put type into a real space and see how the space changes its meaning. Our team walked around campus looking for places with a strong mood, or an interesting way people use them.

The project shifted how I think about type: not just something you read, but something you experience with your whole body.

Three concepts

Week 1

We pitched three directions, each with its own location, moodboard and mockups.

Splash Zone: a ground-floor bathroom, a hazard-sign mark and wet, dripping letters.

Don’t Spiral: a round window where repeating type mimics anxious thoughts.

Sunshine: a warmer, uplifting piece for a sunny second-floor lounge.

Choosing Splash Zone

Week 2

After critique, Splash Zone won: the most playful tone, the strongest tie to its space, and the most immersive. We then tested placement, scale and vinyl at full size.

The final options side by side.

A mockup of the hazard sign on a real stall door.

From mockup to wall

Weeks 2–4

Our first idea was a single hazard sign. Testing it at full size showed it felt like a real sign and got ignored, so we let the letters splash across the whole room instead.

Before

Week 2: gray paper mockups of the logo and drips on the wall.

After

Week 4: blue vinyl letters spilling across the tiles.

  • Went from one sign to letters scattered around the whole room, so you’re surrounded.
  • Switched from gray to a bright blue that reads like water on the tiles.
  • Placed the drips around fixtures and corners, so the type seems to react to the space.
