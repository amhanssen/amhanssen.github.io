Case study · Brand identity · 2025

Paws & Pastries

A brand for a dog-friendly café in Carmel-by-the-Sea, with treats for people and pups: name, logo, menus and a 24 × 36 in poster.

My role

Brand strategy, naming, logo, type system, menus and poster

Timeline

7 weeks · Fall 2025

Course

Graphic Design 2: Making Meaning, with Joe Quackenbush

Tools

Illustrator, InDesign, Photoshop, Figma

The brief

Start with food memories, end with a bone.

Graphic Design 2 asked us to invent a restaurant and build its whole brand. I started with a mind map around food memories, the smells, places and people tied to eating, and kept coming back to slow mornings, pastries and dogs under the table.

That became Paws & Pastries: “Where sweet treats and wagging tails come together.” A café with a shaded patio, water bowls and leash hooks, a dog park out back, weekly events with shelter dogs, and a menu for both people and pups.

Research

Week 1

I set the café in Carmel-by-the-Sea, a storybook village on California’s Monterey Peninsula known for being dog friendly. Two moodboards tested the feeling, and two personas kept the brand grounded in real people.

The location: Carmel-by-the-Sea, a dog-friendly village on the Monterey Peninsula.

Two moodboards: warm pastries, cottage storefronts and dogs at the table, in plum, pink, butter and coral.

Personas: Claire, a local who adopted her dog at one of the café’s events, and Rosa, a college student who works the counter.

What makes customers come back: the patio, the dog park, the community garden and weekly events.

From 100 marks to one

Weeks 2–4

I sketched 100 marks, literal and metaphoric, then ran the name through hundreds of typefaces. The ten strongest went into Illustrator. The winner was a bone holding the name, simple enough to work at any size.

An early sketch: a dog and a pastry in one line.

A type run, testing how the name reads in different letterforms.

The ten refined marks, each testing a different idea.

The dual lockup

Week 4

One mark, two shapes: a horizontal bone for signs and the website, and a stacked version for menus and narrow pieces. I tested both in positive and negative, so the weight holds on any background.

The system

Design decisions

Everything had to feel like a cozy bakery, not a pet store. Deep chocolate keeps it grown-up, pink and butter make it sweet, and patterns built from the logo’s shapes tie every piece together.

Reworking the menu

Weeks 5–7

I set both menus in black and white first, to get the type right, then added color. The first color version was sweet but flat. The final one uses a pattern from the logo, so the menu feels like part of the brand.

Before

First color draft: a scalloped border and a single list.

After

Final menu: the paw pattern frames three columns, with a sleepy-dog illustration.

  • Moved from one column to three, so breakfast, lunch and pastries are easier to scan.
  • Replaced the scalloped border with a paw-print pattern drawn from the logo.
  • Gave the pup menu its own bone-shaped card, so it’s fun to hand to customers.

The brand poster

Week 6

The 24 × 36 in poster is a snapshot of the whole system: logo variations, the brand equation, typefaces, patterns, color palette, menus and applications.

Before

First version: everything on white, so it felt like a spec sheet.

After

Final: the chocolate panels and butter background make the poster feel like the café.

  • Put each part of the system in its own chocolate panel, so the poster reads as one brand, not a list.
  • Added real applications, like tote bags, coffee cups and signage, to show the brand in use.

Reflection

“Move past first-thought solutions.”

What worked

Drawing 100 marks pushed me past the obvious paw print. The bone shape only showed up after the easy ideas ran out.

What I learned

Designing in black and white first made the type stronger. Color is easier to add when the layout already works.

Next time

I’d design more of the café itself, like signs, cups and the patio, to show the brand in its space.
