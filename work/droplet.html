Droplet

A keychain that spins one click every time you refill your water bottle. No app, no streaks, no guilt: just a satisfying click and a number that goes up.

My role

Concept, character, 3D printing, packaging and social posts

Timeline

Fall 2026

Course

Everyday Enhancements · MassArt [check module]

Tools

Bambu Lab A1 3D printer, PLA [add design apps]

The problem

Your brain is thirsty.

Nobody warns you that “I can’t focus” during a 9am lecture might just be your brain running low on water. Students carry bottles everywhere and still lose track of how much they drink.

Hydration apps ask for one more notification and one more streak to feel bad about. Droplet is the opposite: a keychain you clip anywhere, spin at the fountain, and flex your weekly count to absolutely no one but yourself.

From blob to buddy

Character

Droplet started as a simple black silhouette under an arched wordmark. As the keychain took shape, the character grew into the product itself.

Before

Early: a black silhouette with star hands and stubby legs.

After

Final: the keychain hole became the top of the drop, and the counter window sits right next to a big eye.

  • Made the character and the keychain the same shape, so the mascot is the product.
  • Turned the spinning number wheel into a big googly eye, with a small window that shows the count.
  • Added a tongue and purple sneakers to keep it goofy, not clinical.

Reflection

“Just a satisfying click and a number that goes up.”

What worked

Making the character and the product the same thing. The keychain hole, the eye and the counter all became part of Droplet’s face.

What I learned

Physical products need lots of test prints. Small changes in fit decide whether the wheel spins with a satisfying click or not at all.

Next time

I’d give Droplets to classmates for a week and see if counting refills actually makes them drink more.
